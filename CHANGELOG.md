# Changelog

All notable changes to Fantasy Studio are documented here.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

Pre-1.0 versions are internal milestones during the constraint sprint leading to public early access and the V1.0 launch. They're documented here transparently — Fantasy Studio has never been a stealth project.

---

> **Version numbers.** App releases are the GitHub tags (`v1.1.0` … `v1.3.0`). The `1.3.x`–`1.4.x` headings further down are internal pipeline milestones from the spring of 2026, before the app had its own release numbering.

## [Unreleased]

### Added — sentences the studio had never seen (2026-10-22/23)
Three rounds of stress tests built dozens of unusual sentences and gave each kind of place, vehicle and play that came out wrong a builder of its own:
- **Giant rooms**: a tiny hero (an ant, a mouse, a toy) in a kitchen, bedroom, living room, bathroom, library or cellar sees it from the floor, laid out in real metres at the hero's scale.
- **The sea floor** for any sentence set under the water; **islands in the sky** over a sea of cloud, bridged for heroes who cannot fly.
- **Surfing**: swells that roll in and break, a board that rides them, and a new objective, *ride N waves*.
- **Backyards** (house, deck, fence, shed, swing set, tyre swing; snow and a snowman in winter) and **parks** (gravel walks, fountain, benches, lamps, playground, duck pond, picnic tables).
- **Vehicles built in code**: a hot-air balloon you fly (burner and vent) over Cappadocia's fairy chimneys, a six-wheeled planetary rover, a steam locomotive on its line.
- **Kites** that fly downwind on a sagging string; **spacewalks** that float beside the station above the Earth; **skiing** on snow; **hurricanes** that rain.
- **Casting**: the rider rides the mount, the ghost can be the hero, a snowball fight is kids throwing snowballs, a giant robot stays giant, period settings dress their crowds, a creature is never played by a person.
- **Crystal Works, a painted sky** (flagship): a green worldlet now floats in its complement, a violet night with a rose nebula and a warm amber giant over the horizon, cool shadows and warm lights, instead of a green haze. Islets adrift round it: a domed meadow dotted with tufts and small flowers over layered rock narrowing to a point, low trees on top, roots hanging below, water spilling off a rim into the dark, the whole ring drawn in two calls. The neighbouring worlds are hand-painted: Ember Reach's basalt split by glowing lava, Frostline's ice crazed and drifted, The Long Drift's dark plating pricked with lit windows.
- **Paper cranes** (flagship): two skeins of origami cranes, folded in washi cream, faded vermilion and indigo, fly loose V formations round the green worldlet among its islets, beating their paper wings slowly. They ride in the islets' own batch, so the ring still draws in two calls.
- **Viking longships**: "a viking longship raids a foggy coast" sails a clinker-built longship with a dragon prow, shields along the gunwales, oars out and a striped square sail, against longships of its own kind under other colours, on a spruce-and-birch northern coast. Its crew throws spears.
- **Biplanes you fly**: "a mail plane pilot flying over the snowy peaks of the Andes" walked a snowfield. A pilot now flies a biplane built in code (doped-fabric wings with ribs showing, struts and rigging wires, a polished cowl with its cylinder heads, a wooden prop, wheels in spats, a painted tail; AIR MAIL on the flanks and a mail sack up front when there is post to carry). It cruises where the nose points, banks to turn, climbs and dives, and keeps itself above the treetops, over a world grown large enough to fly across.
- **Water towns**: "a paper lantern festival on a river in an old Chinese town at night" built a red-brick English town. A Chinese town on a river or canal is now a water town of the Yangtze delta: whitewashed houses with lattice windows lit amber, dark tile roofs that lift at the corners, stepped gable walls, granite quays and round-arched bridges, canopied boats, weeping willows, a pair of red lanterns at every door and strings of them across the water; a festival sets lotus lanterns drifting down the canal and paper lanterns rising into the night. Broadleaf trees and bamboo round it, not pines.
- **Markers that belong to the place**: the gold and red beams over beehives, letterboxes and lost hikers are gone. A hive is found by its cloud of bees and the ribboned cane beside it (green once checked), a letterbox by the lamp on its bracket, a lost hiker by the red smoke of their flare.
- **The log drive**: "a log driver riding timber down a wild Canadian river" drove an SUV through the woods. A log driver is now a lumberjack standing on a half-sunk spruce log with a pike pole, carried down the river's own channel by the current among the rest of the drive: W poles on, S checks the log, A and D work it across the river; rocks break the surface with white water torn off them, and a rock or a drifting log knocks the log back and sets it rolling. The ride ends at the sawmill on the bank, its wheel turning in the river and its timber stacked against the wall. The north woods (Canada, Alaska, the taiga, the logging camps) grow spruce, pine and birch.
- **Country seen from a cockpit**: a plane's world was a ten-metre forest floor with the ranges only at the rim. It is now landscape for flying: the Andes rise in ridged crests to 150 m with a valley along the route, grey rock and scree, tawny tussock on the valley floors, snow above a ragged line and on the summits, and pines only below the treeline. Farm country under a crop duster lies flat in a surveyed patchwork of ripe wheat, green crop, ploughed earth, stubble and pasture, each field drilled its own way, hedgerows between them and a lone tree here and there, out to an open horizon.
- **The French Quarter**: "a jazz saxophonist playing on a rainy New Orleans street at night" walked between glass towers. New Orleans is now two to four storeys of pastel stucco and old brick, with cast-iron galleries on the street faces: balconies with lace railings at every upper floor, most standing out over the banquette on slender posts, ferns hanging from some.
- **A fish market hall**: "a sushi chef buying fish at a busy Tokyo fish market at dawn" walked a European quay of brick warehouses. A fish market is now a long open-sided shed under a curved corrugated roof on steel columns, wet concrete underfoot, aisles of stalls with white boxes of fish on crushed ice under bare bulbs, whole tuna laid out on some counters, indigo noren dyed with a white fish, turret trucks parked in the aisles and red lanterns along the eaves.
- **A spacewalk on the outside of the station**: "an astronaut fixing a broken solar panel on the outside of a space station" stood on nothing 34 metres under the station with the camera inside the astronaut's helmet, beside a warehouse door labelled "the station". The outside of a station is now a spacewalk: the astronaut floats up to the torn wing.
  - The station has more detail. Its truss is an open lattice (four longerons, frames every three metres, cross-bracing, yellow handrails), not a solid bar.
  - Its modules are wrapped in quilted thermal blankets, stitched panel by panel, one in gold foil.
  - A generated "space station" no longer hangs beside the real one.
- **Crystal Works: hot ground on the ember face**: the ember face was a dull purple-brown with olive grass. It is now a heath of rust and burnt-orange heather over ground split by fissures, charred black with their lips lit orange from the heat below.
- **An old town, not a downtown**: "a street musician playing violin in a snowy Prague square at night" stood between glass offices on a four-lane road. Forty-odd old European cities (Prague, Vienna, Kraków, Bruges, Lisbon, Edinburgh, Florence and their kind) now build as an old town:
  - houses of three to five storeys wall to wall in rendered stucco (ochre, cream, salmon, sage, rose) under steep red-tile roofs, a church tower over them;
  - lanes of granite setts with no paint on them;
  - at its heart, one paved square laid in fans with a baroque column and its gilded saint.
- **Busking**: a street musician plays for the passers-by. Three pitches round the square, each an open case on the cobbles with a hat beside it; hold E to play, notes rise and the coins ring into the case.
- **A ramen shop**: "a chef cooking ramen in a tiny Tokyo restaurant late at night" cooked in a bistro of round tables and potted palms. A ramen kitchen is now:
  - pale hinoki counters, dark slatted walls and a slate floor, red paper lanterns, an indigo noren split over the door, and the menu on hanging wooden plaques;
  - tall stock pots steaming beside the noodle boiler, and a bowl station of chashu, eggs, scallions and nori.
- **A cat up a tree**: "a firefighter rescuing a cat from a tall oak tree in a quiet suburb" was a dirt clearing with the cat on the ground (and, being a firefighter, a burning building nobody asked for). A suburb is now a town of houses, and the cat sits on a limb of a big tree in a front yard with a ladder against the trunk; reach its foot and the cat comes down into your arms.
- **Rowing passengers, a monarch, no flat cards**: a ferryman rowing passengers now rows; a monarch butterfly is a butterfly, not a king; a generated model that came out as a flat picture card (a "milkweed" of black slabs) is left out of the world. A car is offered to steal only in a story of crime or a chase.
- **Photographs, not tokens**: "a scuba diver photographing sea turtles on a coral reef" collected six glowing orbs called "photos". The turtles are now what you find, life size and unlit, and each one is a picture taken: a white flash, the click of a shutter, and "Snap! 2/5 sea turtles photographed".
- **Crystal Works: a salt marsh you can read**: the salt face was soft white airbrush over pale sage, and walking onto it felt like walking into haze. It is now a dark sage marsh with brine creeping along winding channels, white crust dried into cracked plates with lit rims, and samphire going red, its growth sea-purslane green, not pale straw.
- **A train standing in the snow**: "a night train conductor walking along the carriages through a snowy forest" walked a clearing past an overturned van, with nothing to do. Someone on foot beside a train now walks a railway through the trees with the train standing on it: an engine at the head, steam drifting off its chimney, and three to six coaches with their windows lit at night. The walk ends at the engine. (Coach roofs, which lay on their sides in every train game, now arch over the top.)
- **A glassblower's hot shop**: "a glassblower shaping a vase in a glowing workshop" was an explorer at a campfire in a pine wood. A glassblower now works in an open-fronted brick shed: a furnace with its glory hole white-hot, a reheating drum, the marver, the bench with a molten gather on its pipe, a rack of blowpipes, the annealer, and shelves of finished vases, bottles and bowls in coloured glass, all in flickering firelight.
- **A henhouse to raid**: "a fox stealing eggs from a henhouse on a moonlit farm" was a vegetable plot with nothing to steal. Where a sentence keeps hens, a coop on legs now stands beside the way, with a ramp, nest boxes, a chicken-wire run and straw, and the eggs lie in the nest boxes and the straw.
- **A farmers market**: a farmers market is now a village green ringed by market stalls, not a pine wood.
- **Never nothing to do**: four of eight new sentences came back with no goal at all. When the planner writes none, the sentence's verbs now give one: stealing eggs collects them, landing in a field of poppies reaches the poppy field, floating down a river reaches its end, walking along a train reaches the engine. A feeling is no longer a thing to pick up ("collect peace", "collect resonance").
- **Floating on the water**: an otter, a duck or a swan that floats or drifts now lies on the surface instead of swimming along the bottom.
- **Up the lighthouse stair**: "a lighthouse keeper climbing the stairs in a storm to relight the lamp" stepped through the door into a castle's great hall forty metres long, in a tower six metres across, and the walk ended at the door. Inside a lighthouse is now one round room of whitewashed stone, and a stone stair winds up its wall round a thick newel: ninety treads with a brass handrail, deep-set windows on the storm and oil lamps on brackets. It climbs to a landing under the lantern room, where the great lens stands dark. The lamp and its beams over the sea stay out until the keeper reaches the top and lights it. Indoors there is no sea under the floor and no rain falling, and a stair passing over the way out no longer puts you back outside.
- **A snail race along a mossy log**: "a snail racing a beetle across a mossy log" raced across a lawn under a glowing orange hoop seven metres high. A log a small hero goes along or across is now the course: a fallen trunk from start to finish with furrowed bark, cushions of moss, cut ends showing their rings and a few small toadstools, its back the ground the racers run on. Race hoops and the finish line are built to the racers' size.
- **A hill above a seaside town**: "a child flying a kite on a windy hill above a seaside town" was a street grid of brick houses with grass up through the roads. The town is now a village of cottages on the coast, and the path climbs a grassy hill from it to a crown above the beach.
- **Walking the sea floor**: "a deep sea diver walking on the ocean floor past a sunken galleon" swam, bobbed at the surface (the title said "galleon", so the diver was floated like a boat), and saw the seabed's rim break the surface under the stars. The diver now walks the bottom in weighted boots with a helmet lamp lighting the sand ahead, nothing breaks the surface, and the galleon lies broadside at the journey's end.
- **Honey from the hives**: "a beekeeper collecting honey from the hives" picked glowing "honeys" off the path. The honey now comes out of the hives along the lavender rows: hold E as the lid comes up and you lift out a frame of comb.
- **A firefly gathers its swarm**: each firefly found wakes and follows, blinking, so the cloud of lights round the hero grows as the meadow is searched.
- **Packed snow, not a dirt lane**: a winter village's lane is trodden snow with sledge runners and ski grooves down it, not a strip of brown earth.
- **A paper boat in the gutter**: "a paper boat sailing down a rainy gutter in Paris" was a six-metre rowing boat stranded on the asphalt. The hero is now a boat folded from newsprint, a hand long, carried by the rainwater running along the edge of the street, rain rings on the stream, leaves, a bottle cap and a cigarette end drifting with it; A and D steer past what floats, W gives it a push, and the stream ends at a cast-iron storm drain. The camera sits down at the water.
- **Hydroponics on a space station**: "a robot gardener watering plants on a space station" was a station of bunks with nothing growing. When the sentence is about plants, half the modules are grow bays (racks of lettuces three high under magenta grow light), and watering is its own job: trays of wilting seedlings that stand up green as you hold E.
- **Cliff ledges over a glacier**: "a mountain goat leaping between cliff ledges above a glacier" walked a sandy wood. A crag sentence now builds a band of rock shelves climbing to the top, each a leap above the last, a sheer wall on one side and a twenty-metre drop to the glacier on the other; a goat leaps (Space) and scrambles up, and a fall puts it back on the last ledge it stood on.
- **Night markets**: "a lantern-lit night market in Taipei with steaming food stalls" walked an empty neon street. The street the hero starts on is now the market: rows of stalls under striped canopies strung with warm bulbs, signboards (臭豆腐, 小籠包, 珍珠奶茶...), food on the counters, steam rising from the woks and red paper lanterns strung overhead.
- **Sunflower fields** turn to the sun: every head faced away and was backlit olive; they face the sun now, deep gold round dark seed heads, and the wildflowers among them are gold too.
- **Bayous at night**: the black water no longer breaks in white surf at its banks, the night mist is dark air rather than snow, and fireflies blink yellow-green.
- **The high wire**: "a tightrope walker crossing between two skyscrapers high above the city at night" walked the street. Two towers now rise clear of every roof round them, their windows lit, a parapet, water tank and blinking red mast on each roof, a steel cable strung between them with its sag. The walker starts on the first roof with a long balancing pole; W walks the wire, the wind leans the body and A and D lean back against it, and leaning too far is a fall back to the first roof.
- **A grotto of glowing jellyfish**: "a deep sea diver discovering a cave of glowing jellyfish" swam a sunlit reef to a beacon on open sand, and one build had no jellyfish at all. The deep is dark now; the sea floor is levelled under an arch of boulders opening into a domed rock chamber, and the jellyfish (always there when the sentence goes to see them) drift in it, translucent bells lit from within in pink, cyan and violet, pulsing as they swim, some casting their light on the stone.
- **Cenotes**: "an archaeologist exploring a flooded Mayan cenote by torchlight" walked a dark English wood. A cenote is now a sinkhole in the limestone: a round pit open to the sky with sheer walls, a ledge cut into the wall spiralling down from the rim to a pool of clear turquoise water, torches burning along the ledge, vines hanging down the stone, a carved stela by the water, and jungle round the mouth (the Maya lands grow jungle). The pool is its own goal, with no lamp post standing in it.
- **Street vendors**: "a hot dog vendor serving customers on a snowy Chicago street corner" was played by a dog. A food is not a creature; a vendor is made for the game in a padded winter jacket and apron, and carries the food to the customers along the street.
- **Duels**: "a samurai duel in a bamboo forest" made the samurai the enemy and a park ranger the hero. A duel, a joust or a showdown is between two of a kind.
- **A fish market in its city**: in Tokyo (or any city) the market hall now stands in its neighbourhood instead of a forest: an asphalt street round the yard, two and three storeys of shop-houses with roll shutters, vertical kanban signboards, water tanks on the roofs and utility poles with their wires sagging between them, and towers rising out of the haze beyond.
- **Greenhouse domes**: "an astronaut tending a greenhouse garden inside a dome on Mars" was a stone dungeon corridor. A dome, a greenhouse or a biodome is now a geodesic shell of glass panes on a lattice of struts standing in the open (here on red Martian ground), an airlock facing the way you come, and inside raised beds of greens and tomatoes in spokes round hydroponic racks under magenta grow lights.
- **Rowing**: "a lighthouse keeper rowing supplies across a stormy bay at night" walked along the shore. A rower now stands at the oars of a clinker dory laden with crates and a lantern on the stem, afloat on the bay and pitching in the storm's swell; W pulls, S backs water, A and D turn her, and she grounds where the water gives out.
- **Penguins toboggan**: in Antarctica (ice to the horizon, no trees, a dark-rock shore and an icy sea) a penguin waddles, and Shift lays it on its belly to slide.
- **Golden hour**: "lavender fields of Provence at golden hour" stood under a pale noon sky with a white glare where the sun was. A sunset's sun is amber now, its captured sky is graded amber along the horizon and a deeper blue overhead (the light it casts warms with it), and the light shafts take the sun's own colour instead of washing the view white. Lavender bushes are cushions of silvery needles under their purple spikes, not smooth green domes.
- **The tower ladder**: "a firefighter rescuing a cat from a burning apartment building" put the cat on the pavement beside a pale fire. A fire with someone to rescue now burns in a brick or stone block, flames in its real windows, soot up the wall above them and a thick dark plume leaning off the roof. A fire engine stands below, light bar going and outriggers down, its three-section ladder resting its basket on the pavement. Step in and press E: the boom lifts you to the burning window where the cat waits on the sill, the cat hops into the basket, and the ladder brings you both down.

### Fixed
- **A black box over half the frame**: one NaN pixel (the lighthouse beam's shader) went through the bloom blur and came out as a hard black rectangle. The beam is clamped, and bloom now skips any pixel that is not a number.
- **"The pyramids of Giza" built no pyramid**: the goal was plural and only "pyramid" was read.
- Fog is still air: a sentence that names fog or mist and no weather is no longer rained on.
- A "paper lantern" (or a paperboy) no longer turns the whole game into papercraft.
- Badlands, deserts, canyons and other dry country are no longer rained on unless the sentence brings the weather.
- No more palms among the pines: the random cypress draw grows palms only where the place is warm.
- "Searches a snowy slope for three lost hikers" made three plain walk-to goals: a search with the place in between is now a search, each lost hiker found by reaching them under the red smoke of their flare.
- The story is told what the player is told: a beekeeper checks hives, a lumberjack fells trees, a rescuer finds the lost, a firefighter puts out fires (the story used to say "repair" for all of them); and the guide no longer sends the player to gold lights that were removed.
- The Sahara, Arabia and the Gobi grow no cactus; a goal of the sea, or rowing across a bay, lays a coast.
- A person the sentence names by what they do (a saxophonist, a violinist) is made for the game, not swapped for the generic man or dressed up as the explorer; and a character generated lying on its back is stood up rather than thrown away as a bust (the saxophonist was made three times and retired three times).
- Side-on games (the dune look) no longer show a grey void across the bottom of the screen: the orthographic camera's lower rows ran beneath the ground; a dark near ridge stands in front of the lane now, its crest following the dunes.
- A trade word is a person: "vendor" was classed as a vending machine (never rigged, the generic man played the hot dog vendor) and a "hot dog vendor" was sized as a dog; the head noun decides both.
- A cat is a cat's size: a small animal the planner made person-sized (a 1.7 m cat) is sized by its kind, unless the sentence makes it giant.
- Markdown on title cards: a ship's name came through as "\*Sea Serpent\*".
- **Whole plans thrown away**: one out-of-range number in the planner's answer (a 5 cm ant, a 30 m robot) discarded the entire spec; the spec now repairs itself.
- **Insects rigged standing on their tails**: the upright check reads where the body's mass sits.
- **Seconds of boot in every world with stone**: a texture stored in a material's `userData` was JSON-serialised, image and all, on every clone.
- Keyword rules that misfired on unusual sentences: sailing is not a sea battle, a raid is not a car race, a factory you roll through is a place, "orbit" is not an asteroid field, a snowboarder on a salt marsh keeps the marsh.

## [1.3.0] - 2026-10-22

### Added
- **A real Windows installer** (`FantasyStudio-Setup-1.3.0.exe`, about 2 MB) and a first-run setup window that installs its own Python, the engine, the model library and Ollama, every download checked against published SHA-256 checksums. Replaces the PowerShell one-liner.
- **Weather you can see**: rain that lands and splashes, snowstorms that blow, mist that lies in the hollows.
- **Places the sentence names are built**: drowned cities, jungle temples with hidden gardens, castles with towers, harbours, doorsteps for deliveries.
- **A maker per game**: its own minimap style, emblem and typefaces (41 more open-licence families, 95 in all).
- **Drawn worlds get drawn people**; sharper faces; 2K photo-scanned ground and building materials.
- **Crystal Works** (the flagship) reads as a small world from orbit, with a new build mark and grass.

### Fixed
- The scene is antialiased where it is drawn (the ambient-occlusion pass rendered the scene without multisampling), and forest level-of-detail changes no longer stipple.
- A multi-second freeze at Start in many worlds (a light budget that changed the shader count on the first frame).

## [Development log] - July 2026

### Added — Phase 60+61: 1:1 reference texturing + new game genres (2026-07-11)
- **Texture v2 (`texture_v2.py`, FS_TEX_V2 default on)** — kills the
  "half-stretched face/body" on CPU-generated heroes. The v1 flat side
  projection mapped face/chest/rump onto a thin strip of photo pixels; v2
  bakes a smart-UV atlas (numpy rasterizer, no Cycles), keeps the photo where
  the surface faced the reference camera, and pyramid-inpaints the smear
  zones (~20% of the surface on bear/cat). Verified by front-render A/B:
  streak bands gone; flanks stay 1:1 with the reference. Companion fix:
  skin v2's geodesic graph is now WELDED BY POSITION so atlas UV-seam vertex
  splits can't fragment it (the harness caught a cat morph regression from
  exactly that — fixed, all gates pass).
- **Battle royale (`eliminate` objective)** — "battle royale / last one
  standing / eliminate all rivals" spawns hostile rivals of the player's own
  kind plus a SHRINKING STORM ZONE (translucent wall + ground ring, closes
  over 150 s, 1 HP/s outside). Win = all rivals down. Verified live in
  browser: 3 rivals, quest "Last one standing — eliminate 3 rivals 0/3",
  zero errors.
- **Sports (`score` objective)** — "soccer / score N goals" spawns an arcade
  ball (gravity, bounce, rolling drag, world-bounds), walk into it to kick
  (Shift = power), goalposts at the level goal, GOAL! burst + counter, win at
  N. Verified live in browser: "Score 2 goals — 0/2", ball physics running
  clean during play.
- Both genres wired end-to-end: spec Literal, LLM grammar + keyword fallback
  (works with Ollama down), runtime, quest log/HUD. Style/view snapshot
  re-baselined for the intended runtime change; morph gate untouched.
- **Godot multiplayer starting point** — every Godot export now ships
  MULTIPLAYER_GUIDE.md: the 5-step Godot 4 high-level networking path
  (ENetMultiplayerPeer, MultiplayerSpawner/Synchronizer, per-peer authority,
  server-authoritative mission RPCs) mapped to this project's player.gd/
  mission.gd structure. Honest scope: web builds stay single-player; Godot
  is the multiplayer route.

### Added — Phase 57+58: orientation telemetry + gated retopo keystone (2026-07-11)
- **Phase 57 (orientation)**: auto-fix was REJECTED BY EVIDENCE — two
  independent geometric leg-gap detectors false-positived on known-good rigs
  (one flipped a correct polar bear twice; caught by visual render checks,
  file restored + re-verified). Ships as detection-only telemetry
  (`verify_glb_orientation`, FS_ORIENT_VERIFY, off by default) + a visual QA
  tool (`scripts/_render_pose_check.py`). Orientation stays guaranteed where
  it is provable: the bake-time 24-view silhouette gate vs the reference +
  feet-down/head-up priors + rig-cache mtime invalidation.
- **Phase 58 (retopo keystone, FS_RETOPO=1 opt-in)**: `app/game_export/
  retopo.py` — voxel remesh (manifold/watertight/shard-fusing, density-guarded)
  + hygiene pass + largest-island filter + best-effort QuadriFlow + UV/material
  transfer, inserted before rigging in both anim bakes; failure restores the
  original mesh. HONEST FINDINGS: Blender 5.1's QuadriFlow rejects the
  voxel-remeshed TRELLIS heroes ("needs manifold/consistent normals") even at
  0 non-manifold verts / 1 island / consistent normals — probed exhaustively
  (symmetry off, triangulated, mesh-doctor); a cube passes in the same
  session. Voxel-only keystone measured MIXED on the harness: bear 0.702→0.668
  (better), cat 0.613→0.749 (worse), fox 0.320→1.725 (fur is hundreds of
  legitimate alpha-strip shells — island filter + voxel destroy them).
  Therefore OPT-IN ONLY; library keeps the Phase 54 rigs. Upgrade path:
  standalone instant-meshes (BSD-3) or newer Blender + per-pattern gating —
  GPU-day. Bridge ops learned: quadriflow needs a long-timeout call (420 s);
  never read_factory_settings through the bridge (kills the addon socket).

### Added — Phase 53+54: quality harness + quadruped skin v2 (morphing fix, part 1) (2026-07-11)
- **Quality harness** (`scripts/quality_harness.py`): the regression gate for all
  character/scene quality work. `morph` mode measures p95 EDGE STRETCH on the
  animated library rigs via headless Blender — wrong-bone skinning weights
  stretch edges between limbs, so this is the "morphing" defect as a number.
  `styles` mode hashes fixed-seed exports per style/view preset to catch drift.
  Baselines live in `renders/quality_baseline/` (gitignored); `--compare`
  fails on any regression, so every quality change is proven, not eyeballed.
- **Quadruped skin v2** (`app/game_export/skin_v2.py`, flag `FS_SKIN_V2`,
  default on, per-step auto-fallback to the untouched Euclidean path):
  1. *Same-side/same-end constraints* — a left-leg bone can never claim a
     clearly-right-side vertex (ported from the proven biped mocap skin).
  2. *Geodesic surface distance* for leg bones — pure numpy+heapq multi-source
     Dijkstra over the mesh edge graph (bridge Python has no scipy). Surface
     distance makes the inner thigh "far" from the other leg even though it is
     physically near — the root cause of cross-limb weight bleed.
  3. *Laplacian weight smoothing* + max-4-influence clamp.
- **Measured (harness, p95 edge stretch, lower=better):**
  polar_bear 0.934 → 0.702 (−25%) · cat 0.737 → 0.613 (−17%) ·
  fox 0.698 → 0.320 (−54%) · man 0.481 (untouched biped path, unchanged).
  Visual A/B mid-run confirms: legs read as legs instead of sheared sheets.

### Fixed — Phase 53: rig cache invalidates when its static mesh is re-baked (2026-07-08)

### Fixed — Phase 52: quadruped feet-down orientation prior (2026-07-08)
- **The bug**: the generated polar bear shipped BELLY-UP in-game (legs in the
  air) at silhouette IoU 0.34. Root cause: the 24-view silhouette orientation
  gate had geometric up/down priors for BIPEDS (head-up) and VEHICLES
  (wheels-down), but NONE for quadrupeds — and the 2D silhouette centre-of-mass
  tiebreak can't tell a chunky animal's back from its belly (both outlines look
  nearly identical), so a belly-up pick sailed through.
- **Fix (scalable, all quadrupeds)**: added a `quad_feet_down` geometric prior
  to `_orient_hero_by_reference`, mirroring the biped one. After the silhouette
  pick + azimuth-normalize, it measures the LEG-GAP (belly clearance between the
  front/back leg pairs along the body axis, and the left/right leg split across
  it) in the bottom vs top Z-slabs. If the leg-gap is at the TOP, the animal is
  belly-up → roll 180° about Y (the body long axis), preserving the heading the
  silhouette already chose. Reference-independent; fixes bear, dog, horse,
  tiger, etc. — not a per-asset override. Wired in `bake.py` for
  `pattern == "quadruped"`.
- Verified: re-baked the polar bear (raw mesh cached) → renders standing
  feet-down, head forward. `composer.py` + `bake.py` compile clean.
- KNOWN (GPU-day, not this fix): the fur texture is still single-view baked, so
  off-camera faces show brown/tan patches — the real fix is multi-view texturing
  (needs a GPU). Tracked separately.

### Fixed — Phase 51: hero casting never crosses species (2026-07-08)
- **The bug**: "a polar bear that must defeat 3 knights to reach the sacred
  igloo" spent ~30-60 min attempting to generate the bear on CPU, the attempt
  failed, and the casting ladder silently fell back to `library.resolve("man")`

  degrades to a quadruped (polar bear → wolf), a vehicle to a vehicle, an
  aquatic to a whale — NEVER a man. Both blind `resolve("man")` fallbacks in
  the player-casting ladder now route through it, with a loud, self-healing
  note: "Couldn't build 'polar bear' yet — needs a GPU; cast 'wolf' as a
  stand-in; re-run once your GPU is in to get the real one."
- **Design intent preserved**: the generate-then-place path stays PRIMARY and
  is always attempted — CPU generation works (~25-30 min once, then cached; the

  a genuine generation failure, so a hero is never a man-with-a-sword, but the

  of this fix wrongly gated generation behind a GPU; reverted.)
- Verified: `nearest()` maps polar bear/grizzly/tiger → wolf, ferrari → car,
  dolphin → whale, cat → cat; TripoSR confirmed available in the backend venv;
  both edited files compile clean.
- **Fix 3 — a bridge hiccup no longer discards a generated mesh**: root-cause

- ENV_PRESET running on top of forced-environment imports caused dual styling (e.g. desert preset color cast over a placed mountain asset). ENV_PRESET now skipped when `forced_environment_id` is set

## [1.3.5] - 2026-03

### Fixed
- BLEND_DEDUP triple-write per child invalidated Object refs → StructRNA crash → builder fallback to placeholder scene. Replaced with a 5-phase transactional flow (gather → reparent → settle → restore-matrix → validate → delete) with abort-on-validation-failure
- Vehicle orientation gate: rotate -90°X only when Z is the longest axis. Skip for asset_type `vehicle`. Stops low-and-wide cars getting flipped on import
- Multi-hit raycast for terrain placement: prefers top-facing surface normals (z > 0.1) so heroes don't land on the side of a dune
- Library reclassification: `lib_desert_desert_landscape` shape_class `flat_map` → `3d_terrain`
- HERO_VERIFY gained two structural checks: `oriented_correctly` (hard-fails when bbox.max_axis is wrong for the asset type), `grounded` (warn-only)
