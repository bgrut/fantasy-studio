# Quality targets — what "good" means, and where we actually are

Written 2026-09-04. The reference points are the user's own: **Fortnite** for
cartoon, **Call of Duty / Assassin's Creed** for realism, **Higgsfield
Supercomputer** and **Reallusion AI Studio** for the generative pipeline.

This file exists because "it looks papery" kept being the right complaint and
the wrong diagnosis three times running. Everything below is measured where a
measurement exists, so progress is arguable against a number instead of a
feeling.

---

## The one-line diagnosis

**Papery = missing depth cues.** Flat even lighting, no occlusion contact, no
surface texture and posterised luminance all say "cut paper" to the eye no
matter how good the colours are. Every cartoon complaint so far has traced to
one of those four.

---

## Cartoon (reference: Fortnite)

Fortnite is **not** a comic-book filter. It is stylised PBR: saturated albedo,
real directional shadows, heavy ambient occlusion, clean high-poly
silhouettes, and the lighting left intact to carry volume.

| | reference | ours (2026-09-04) |
|---|---|---|
| luminance | full range, lit | WAS posterised to 4 bands -> fixed, bands off |
| outlines | none | WAS thick ink -> removed |
| ground | textured | WAS a single flat painted colour -> now textured |
| ambient occlusion | strong | on, intensity raised 3.1 -> 4.4 for cartoon |
| character mesh | high-poly, clean silhouette | **45k tris, claw hands, blob feet — OPEN** |
| ground cover | dense | tufts (3 blades) — better, still sparse/chunky |
| sky | deep, coloured | clamped to a real blue; **FOG still washes the horizon — OPEN** |

### Open
- **Character mesh quality.** The single most-looked-at object in the product.
- **Grass density and blade scale** — reads chunky and claw-like up close.
- **Sky.** A "sunny meadow" renders under a grey gradient.
- **Rim lighting.** Cheap, and a large part of why stylised characters pop.

## Realism (reference: COD, Assassin's Creed)

| | reference | ours |
|---|---|---|
| film grain | subtle, deliberate | fixed: was being SHARPENED ~5x into paper grain |
| ground texture | tight, anisotropic | rock/soil/sand/stone, max anisotropy — good |
| terrain | fine, sculpted | **3.1 m per polygon (48x48 over 150m) — OPEN** |
| characters | scanned/hand-authored | TRELLIS + auto-rig — **the ceiling** |
| physics | full rigid body, ragdoll, vehicles | 40 pushable props, no ragdoll — OPEN |
| shadows | cascaded, contact | PCFSoft, 2048 — needs audit |

### Open
- **Terrain resolution.** 3.1 m polygons are why landforms read as folded card.
- **Ragdoll**, vehicles, deformable ground (the snowboarder reference).
- **Character meshes.** Same ceiling as cartoon, felt harder here.

## 2D (topdown / side)

## The honest strategic note

Physics and skinning were NOT the ceiling — Blender bone-heat via a watertight
voxel proxy is the same technique Reallusion sells as CC Wrap, and it measures
clean. The ceiling is **mesh generation**. Higgsfield's characters look good
because they are generated by paid models; ours are TRELLIS output. That is
the one place a bring-your-own-key option would change the output *category*
rather than merely improve it. Everything else on this page is reachable on
the free local stack.
